\shead{Research interests} I am applying to pursue a PhD in \program{} at \school{} to study how the design of social programs affects labour market outcomes.

\shead{Background} After First Class Honours in Economics and Finance at the University of Melbourne and three years as a senior analyst at the Reserve Bank of Australia, I now work as a predoctoral researcher for Associate Professor Manasi Deshpande. Using the staggered rollout of the NDIS, Australia's disability support program, we find that its marginal entrants into disability work are low-income, lower-skilled women with modest earnings gains.

\shead{Current research} I study two levers for raising labour supply in high-demand sectors: raising wages, whose employment effect is ambiguous because it also raises firm labour costs (e.g., \cite{dalbo_finan_rossi2013}), and subsidising education, which lowers fixed costs of entry \citep{schoonover2026}. I compare Victoria's `Free TAFE', which offers free courses for priority occupations, with a federally funded 15 per cent wage increase for aged care workers \citep{fwc_agedcare2022}. Using administrative data and a difference-in-differences, I find that Free TAFE generated a large and immediate increase in enrolments, strongest among young women previously on income support. I am now estimating employment and wage responses.

\shead{Future research} More broadly, I study how support beyond income assistance shapes labour outcomes. Next, I will examine Victoria's free IVF initiative, which lowers a fixed cost of having children rather than replacing lost income \citep{gallen2026}.
