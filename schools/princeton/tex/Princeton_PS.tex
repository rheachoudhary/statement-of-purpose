When I was eight, my parents moved our family from the Middle East to Australia to give my brother and me a better future. When my Dad struggled to find work, they made the tough decision to live apart: my Mum raised us in Australia effectively as a single parent while my Dad worked long hours to send money home.
\psbreak
I think a lot about how important my family provided an informal safety net that allowed me to take risks and pursue my career aspirations. Getting through the door also required government support: I went to a public university, used government-issued loans and relied on income assistance. Nontheless, I've also seen both fall short. I watched as my brother was unemployed for over three years and needed more than just the imperfect income assistance provided by unemployment insurance. And moral support can only go so far.
\psbreak
These experiences have strongly motivated my decision to pursue graduate study, because I want to answer important questions like these that affect people's lives, and are front-of-mind within the academic community as well as in policymaking.
\psbreak
I believe my personal background greatly enriches Princeton's scholarly community. On the research side, my professional experience in policymaking and predoctoral research helps me construct rigorous, frontier research questions and communicate findings to a policy audience. On the personal side, I want to create a supportive community
where my graduate school peers and I can support one another to achieve our collective goals.
