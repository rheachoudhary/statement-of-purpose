\shead{Research interests} I am interested in questions at the intersection of public and labour economics, specifically the effects of public assistance and social program design on labour market outcomes.

\shead{Background} After completing my undergraduate degree with Honours at the University of Melbourne and three years at the Reserve Bank of Australia, I now work as a predoctoral researcher for Associate Professor Manasi Deshpande. We study Australia's National Disability Insurance Scheme, a first-of-its-kind disability support program, and its effects on labour supply to disability care.

\shead{Current independent research} I am currently studying the relative merits of education subsidies versus minimum wage increases to increase labour supply in high-demand sectors. I do this using a difference-in-differences comparing an education subsidy in Australia that made only certain courses free, to a federally funded wage increase for aged care workers. 

\shead{Future research} I am interested in studying non-financial forms of government assistance and their labour market effects. For instance, I would like to examine a `free IVF' initiative in Australia (which lowers costs of delaying childbirth) and its effects on childbirth and therefore labour market decisions compared to parental income assistance.
